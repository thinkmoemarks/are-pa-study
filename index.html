12 Mnemonics to Memorize

"Thirty-two, walk on through"
32" = minimum clear door opening width (ADA)
"Ramp at twelve, no steeper delve"
1:12 = maximum ramp slope (1" rise per 12" run)
"Thirty rise, then rest your eyes"
30" = max ramp rise before a landing is required
"Thirty-six to pass, that aisle's got class"
36" = minimum clear ramp width
"Five by five, the landing's alive"
60" x 60" = ramp landing at direction changes
"Eighteen past the latch on the pull side catch"
18" clear maneuvering space on pull side of door, latch side
"Point-three for stairs, point-two elsewhere"
0.3"/occ stair egress width, 0.2"/occ other egress (unsprinklered)
"CIA finds the Q"
Q = C × i × A (Rational Method for stormwater runoff)
"Eight wide standard, eleven for the van"
8' accessible parking, 11' van-accessible, both need 5' aisle
"FAR = Floor over Area of the Region"
FAR = Total Floor Area ÷ Total Lot Area
"South-facing gets the sun embracing"
In Northern Hemisphere, south facades get maximum solar exposure
"One in six is van-equipped"
1 of every 6 accessible parking spaces must be van-accessible

Egress Width Calculations

Width = Occupants × Factor
Factor depends on component type and whether building is sprinklered
ComponentStandardSprinklered + voice alarm
Stairways0.3" per occupant0.2" per occupant
All other egress (corridors, doors, ramps)0.2" per occupant0.15" per occupant
Lower factors need both: the building must be fully sprinklered AND have an emergency voice/alarm communication system (IBC 1005.3). Sprinklers alone keep 0.3" and 0.2". Not allowed for Group H or I-2.

Example Calculation

Problem: An unsprinklered office floor is 15,000 SF. How wide must the stairway be?

Step 1: Occupant load = 15,000 ÷ 150 = 100 occupants

Step 2: Stair width = 100 × 0.3" = 30"

Step 3: But IBC minimum stair width = 44" (for 50+ occupants), so use 44"

Always check: calculated width vs. code minimum — use whichever is GREATER.

Building Analysis & Programming (37–43% of PA)

This is the biggest section of the exam. NCARB's objectives ask you to evaluate a new or existing building against the program, use reports to inform it, prioritize program components, work out spatial and functional relationships, check whether the budget and schedule fit the scope, pick building and structural systems, and read the diagrams that show all of it.

The Programming Process

"Go Find Clever New Problems"
Goals → Facts → Concepts → Needs → Problem statement (the Problem Seeking method)

Space Program Math

Gross = Net ÷ Efficiency
Efficiency = net assignable ÷ gross. Grossing factor = gross ÷ net = 1 ÷ efficiency.
EfficiencyGrossing factor
85%1.18
80%1.25
75%1.33
60%1.67
50%2.00

Mixed program: 18,000 NASF offices at 80% plus 6,000 NASF labs at 55%.

Offices: 18,000 ÷ 0.80 = 22,500 GSF

Labs: 6,000 ÷ 0.55 = 10,909 GSF

Total = 33,409 GSF

Gross up each space type at its own efficiency, then add. Don't average the percentages.

Budget & Schedule Feasibility

Total = Construction + Soft Costs + Contingency
Construction = gross SF × cost per SF. On the exam, use the percentages the question gives.

Problem: The client has $5,000,000 total. Soft costs are 25% of construction and contingency is 10% of construction. Construction runs $325/SF. How big can the building be?

Step 1: Construction = 5,000,000 ÷ 1.35 = $3,703,704

Step 2: Area = 3,703,704 ÷ 325 = 11,396 GSF

Divide by (1 + all the percentages). Dividing $5M straight by $325 overstates the area by about 35%.

Essential Formulas for PA

FAR (Floor Area Ratio)

FAR = Total Floor Area ÷ Total Lot Area
Unitless ratio. FAR of 2.0 means you can build 2× the lot area in total floor space.

Example: Lot = 10,000 SF, FAR = 3.0 → Max floor area = 30,000 SF

Could be 3 floors × 10,000 SF, or 6 floors × 5,000 SF, etc.

Lot Coverage

Coverage = Footprint ÷ Lot Area × 100
Usually expressed as a percentage. A 60% max coverage means 40% must be open.

Building Efficiency (Net-to-Gross Ratio)

Efficiency = Net Usable Area ÷ Gross Floor Area
Typical ranges: Office 80–85%, Residential 75–80%, Hospital 55–65%, Lab 50–60%
Building TypeTypical Efficiency
Office80–85%
Residential75–80%
Retail75–80%
Hospital55–65%
Laboratory50–60%
School60–70%

Example: Client needs 40,000 SF net usable office space. Efficiency = 82%.

Gross area needed = 40,000 ÷ 0.82 = 48,780 SF

Occupant Load

Occupants = Floor Area ÷ Load Factor
Use gross or net area depending on the occupancy type per IBC Table 1004.5

Stormwater — Rational Method

Q = C × i × A
Q = peak discharge (cfs)  |  C = runoff coefficient (0–1)  |  i = rainfall intensity (in/hr)  |  A = drainage area (acres)
Surface TypeRunoff Coefficient (C)
Rooftops / pavement0.85–0.95
Gravel surfaces0.40–0.60
Lawns (flat, sandy soil)0.05–0.10
Lawns (steep, clay soil)0.25–0.35
Forest / wooded0.05–0.25
"CIA finds the Q"
C = Coefficient, i = Intensity, A = Area → Q = peak runoff. Higher C (more impervious) = more runoff.

Slope

Slope = Rise ÷ Run
Express as ratio (1:12), fraction, or percent (8.33%). Multiply decimal by 100 for percent.

Ramp Length

Length = Rise × 12
At max 1:12 slope. For a 30" rise: 30 × 12 = 360" = 30 ft of ramp run.

Parking Ratio

Spaces = Building SF ÷ Ratio (per zoning)
Example: zoning requires 1 space per 300 SF of office. 15,000 SF office = 50 spaces.

Practice Quiz — 15 Questions

Test yourself. Pick your answer, then click "Grade My Quiz" at the bottom.

1. An unsprinklered office building has a floor area of 30,000 gross SF. What is the occupant load per IBC 2021?

30,000 SF ÷ 150 SF/person (IBC 2021 Business) = 200 occupants. Note: Under the older 100 SF/person factor it would be 300 — know which edition you're being tested on.

2. What is the minimum clear width of a ramp per ADA?

ADA requires a minimum 36" clear width for ramps. 32" is the door clear width — don't mix them up!

3. A lot is 20,000 SF with a FAR of 2.5. What is the maximum total floor area?

FAR = Total Floor Area ÷ Lot Area. So max floor area = 20,000 × 2.5 = 50,000 SF.

4. Using the rational method, a 5-acre site has C=0.6 and rainfall intensity of 3 in/hr. What is Q?

Q = C × i × A = 0.6 × 3 × 5 = 9 cfs.

5. What is the egress width factor for stairways in an unsprinklered building?

0.3" per occupant for stairways (unsprinklered). Sprinklered with an emergency voice/alarm system = 0.2". For corridors: 0.2" standard, 0.15" sprinklered with voice alarm.

6. What is the maximum door opening force for an accessible interior door?

5 lbs max opening force for interior accessible doors. Fire doors may require more but must have closers set to minimum force.

7. A parking lot has 200 total spaces. How many must be accessible?

Per ADA Table 208.2: 151–200 total spaces = 6 accessible spaces required, with 1 of those being van-accessible.

8. Which facade receives the most intense afternoon heat gain?

West gets intense afternoon sun at a low angle (hard to shade). South gets more total solar but at a higher angle (easier to control with overhangs).

9. A client needs 50,000 SF of net usable hospital space. At 60% efficiency, what gross area is needed?

Gross = Net ÷ Efficiency = 50,000 ÷ 0.60 = 83,333 SF. Hospitals have low efficiency due to corridors, mechanical, and support spaces.

10. Contour lines forming V-shapes near a stream point in which direction?

At streams/valleys, contour V's point UPSTREAM. At ridges, V's point DOWNHILL. This is a classic ARE question.

11. The pull side of a door requires how much clearance on the latch side?

18" on pull side, 12" on push side (with closer and latch). This space lets a wheelchair user reach the handle while clearing the door swing.

12. What is the max travel distance to an exit in a sprinklered business occupancy?

300 ft for a sprinklered Business (B) occupancy per IBC Table 1017.2. Unsprinklered B = 200 ft. Most other occupancies (A, E, F-1, M, R, S-1) top out at 250 ft sprinklered.

13. A 10,000 SF lot with max lot coverage of 60%. Building is 3 stories. What is the max building footprint?

Lot coverage limits the FOOTPRINT, not total floor area. 10,000 × 0.60 = 6,000 SF max footprint. Total floor area would be 6,000 × 3 = 18,000 SF (but that's FAR, not coverage).

14. An assembly space with tables and chairs is 3,000 net SF. What is the occupant load?

Assembly unconcentrated (tables & chairs) = 15 net SF/person. 3,000 ÷ 15 = 200 occupants.

15. The ADA toilet seat height must be between:

ADA requires toilet seat height of 17" to 19" above the finished floor, measured to the top of the seat.
